Pali Aike National Park protects a unique volcanic landscape in Patagonia, with craters, cinder cones, lava caves, and rock formations that evoke lunar landscapes.

Located about 200 km north of Punta Arenas, near the border with Argentina, the park is a testament to the intense volcanic activity that shaped the region.

The name Pali Aike means desolate place in the Tehuelche language, reflecting the remote and wild appearance of the landscape.

The park also has archaeological importance: it contains one of the oldest human habitation sites in Patagonia, dating back more than 11,000 years.
