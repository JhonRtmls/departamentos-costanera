The Sara Braun Museum is one of the most emblematic buildings in Punta Arenas, built between 1895 and 1905 by the wealthy Braun family, one of the most influential in the history of Chilean Patagonia.

Declared a National Monument, this European-style palace is preserved in its original state, allowing visitors to immerse themselves in the life of high society in Magallanes at the beginning of the 20th century.

The museum displays original furniture from the period, personal objects of the Braun family, and an art collection that reflects the splendor of the golden age of livestock farming in Patagonia.

The gardens surrounding the palace are an additional attraction, featuring plant species brought from Europe that adapted to the southern climate.
